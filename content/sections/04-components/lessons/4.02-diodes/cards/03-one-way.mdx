exam_fact: A diode conducts in one direction only, and only when forward biased. Its electrodes are the anode and cathode; electrons flow from cathode to anode. In series with the positive lead it gives reverse polarity protection.

A diode has two electrodes: the **anode** and the **cathode** (the end with the band).

- It conducts in **one direction only**. To conduct it must be **forward biased**: anode more positive than cathode by about 0.6 V. **Reverse biased**, it blocks.
- **Electrons** flow from **cathode to anode**. (Conventional current, drawn by the arrow in the symbol, goes the other way.)
- Put a diode in series with the **positive power lead** and reversed supply leads just meet a blocked diode: **reverse polarity protection**. It doesn't protect against spikes or overcurrent; that's a fuse's job.
