exam_fact: In a basic CW transmitter the oscillator runs at the transmitted frequency. A driver/buffer stops load changes from shifting the oscillator's frequency. The power amplifier increases output power. Every stage runs on DC.
---
1. **Oscillator**: makes the signal, already **at the transmitted frequency** (at low power).
2. **Driver/buffer**: amplifies a little and, importantly, isolates the oscillator. Without it, changes in the load (like keying the amplifier) would **shift the oscillator's frequency**.
3. **Power amplifier**: **increases the output power**. It doesn't change the frequency or clean up chirps.

Every stage is powered by **direct current** from the power supply.
