exam_fact: A conductor's magnetic field is proportional to its current. Induced voltage peaks when current changes fastest. Coupling is greatest between wires that are close and parallel. Warm laminations show transformer losses.
---
- Current in a wire makes a magnetic field whose strength is **directly proportional to the current**.
- A changing field **induces** a voltage; the voltage is largest when the current is changing at its **greatest rate**. Steady DC induces nothing, which is why transformers need AC.
- Coupling between two wires is strongest when they're **close and parallel**.
- Permanent magnets are made from **steel** (copper, aluminum and brass aren't magnetic).
- Real transformers lose some energy as heat: **warm iron laminations** prove the transfer isn't perfect.
